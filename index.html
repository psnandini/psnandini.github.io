Nandini Sekar

Interpreting neural networks, biological and artificial

PhD Candidate, UC Berkeley – UCSF Joint Graduate Program in Bioengineering

How do neural networks represent internal states like mood, and how can we read them out and steer them?

I study how neural networks, biological and artificial, represent internal states such as mood and affect. I decode these states from high-dimensional neural activity, find the circuits that carry them, and test them causally by intervening on the network.

In human neural activity, I have found multiday rhythms that lead mood and forecast it weeks ahead. In the neural activity of language models, I have found that a user's frustration changes whether the model holds or abandons its answers, a signal read out at a specific depth inside the network.

My work spans machine learning, interpretability and AI safety, from long-term recordings of the human brain to the internals of large language models. I work with Dr. Edward Chang and Dr. Ankit Khambhati at UCSF.

I am a 2023 inaugural Quad Fellow and a UC Berkeley Kavli Fellow.

AI safety · Red-teaming

A hidden 16-token clock that sabotages a language model

With an empty system prompt, Dolos-1.5B deletes content on a 16-token clock. We traced it to three attention heads and built an audit that finds it without the base model.

AI safety · Interpretability

Ambient negative affect makes language models defend their answers

Frustration in a conversation makes models entrenched when right and harder to correct when wrong, and the signal is read out at mid-depth.

How does post-training create affect-driven behaviour?

Planned: tracing the effect through open checkpoints at every post-training stage (base, SFT, DPO, RL), auditing their reward models, and testing whether targeted preference training removes the mechanism or only hides it.
